What Is a Pet Ultrasound and How Does It Work?

When your dog skips meals or your cat suddenly hides and stops grooming, you know something is wrong, but you can't see what. A pet ultrasound helps answer that question without surgery.

Ultrasound uses harmless sound waves to build a live picture of your pet's organs. A small probe is moved gently over the skin and your veterinarian watches the screen to check the size, shape and texture of each organ. It uses no radiation and for most pets it is a calm, quiet appointment.

That is why a pet ultrasound in Langley BC is one of the most useful tools in modern veterinary imaging. Where an X-ray shows the outline of organs, ultrasound shows what is going on inside them: the layers of the stomach wall, the structure of the liver, fluid around the heart or in the belly and even a developing litter.

When Is a Pet Ultrasound Needed? 7 Signs to Watch For

Not every sick day needs imaging. But if you notice any of the signs below, especially if they last more than a day or two or keep coming back, ask your vet whether an ultrasound is the right next step.

  1. Repeated vomiting or diarrhea. One upset stomach is common. Weeks of on-and-off vomiting can point to a foreign object, inflammation or a growth that only imaging can find.
  2. Unexplained weight loss or a poor appetite. Pets that are eating normally but losing weight, or refusing food altogether, often need a closer look at the liver, kidneys, pancreas and intestines.
  3. A swollen or painful belly. A tight, bloated or tender abdomen can be caused by fluid, a mass or an organ problem. Ultrasound can show the cause quickly.
  4. Abnormal bloodwork or urine tests. Raised liver or kidney values, or blood in the urine, tell your vet something is wrong but not what or where. Ultrasound fills in that gap.
  5. Drinking and urinating much more than usual. This can be linked to kidney disease, bladder stones, diabetes or other conditions that are easier to confirm when the organs are seen directly.
  6. Something unclear on an X-ray. If a radiograph shows a shadow or an unusual shape, ultrasound is often the follow-up that explains it.
  7. A suspected pregnancy. Whether you are planning a litter or an accidental mating happened, a scan can confirm pregnancy and check on the babies' heartbeats.

If your pet is in sudden severe pain, has a hard swollen belly, is vomiting repeatedly and cannot keep water down, or is collapsing, treat it as an emergency and call a vet right away rather than waiting for a scheduled scan.

Abdominal Ultrasound for Pets: What Can Your Vet See?

An abdominal ultrasound for pets is the most common type of scan and it gives your vet a close look at most of the organs in the belly in a single visit. It can be used to check:

  • Liver and gallbladder: changes in size or texture, cysts, masses or blocked bile flow

  • Kidneys and bladder: stones, infection signs, swelling or growths

  • Stomach and intestines: thickened walls, foreign objects or inflammation

  • Spleen and pancreas: masses, enlargement or inflammation

  • Uterus and prostate: infections, pregnancy or enlargement

  • Free fluid in the abdomen: which can signal bleeding, infection or another underlying problem

Ultrasound can also guide a fine needle to take a tiny sample from an organ or mass, so your vet can get answers without major surgery. This is one reason a dog ultrasound or cat ultrasound is often a turning point in a diagnosis: it can move you from "something is wrong" to "here is what we are dealing with and what we can do."

Cat Pregnancy Ultrasound in Langley (Dogs Too)

If your cat has a rounder belly, a sudden change in appetite or nesting behaviour, you may be wondering whether kittens are on the way. A cat pregnancy ultrasound in Langley can confirm it early and the same goes for dogs.

Here is what a pregnancy scan can tell you:

  • Whether your pet is pregnant at all , which rules out other causes of a swollen belly, such as a uterine infection

  • Whether the babies have heartbeats , a reassuring sign that development is on track

  • Early signs of trouble , so you and your vet can act sooner if something looks wrong

Timing matters. Ultrasound can generally detect pregnancy a few weeks after mating, but your vet will advise the best window for your pet. It is also not the best tool for counting puppies or kittens. An X-ray later in pregnancy is usually more accurate for that, which is one more reason to plan ahead with your vet rather than guess.

Ultrasound vs. X-Ray vs. Bloodwork: Which Veterinary Imaging in Langley Does Your Pet Need?

No single test answers every question, which is why vets often combine them. Here is how they fit together:

  • Bloodwork and urine tests show how organs are functioning and flag infection, inflammation or imbalances. They are usually the first step.

  • X-rays (radiographs) show bones, the chest, the heart outline, and large changes in organ size or position. They are quick and very good at spotting broken bones, swallowed objects that show up on film, and lung changes.

  • Ultrasound shows the inside of soft organs, how they are moving and whether there is fluid or a mass. It is the go-to when tests suggest a problem but the cause is unclear.

In practice, veterinary imaging in Langley often follows a simple path: bloodwork or an exam raises a question, an X-ray gives the big picture and ultrasound answers the detailed one. What to Expect at Your Pet's Ultrasound Appointment

Knowing what happens takes a lot of the worry out of the day.

Before the visit

Your clinic may ask you to hold off on food for several hours beforehand, because an empty stomach gives a clearer picture. Follow the exact instructions your vet gives you, and keep fresh water available unless told otherwise. For some scans, such as the bladder, you may be asked not to let your pet urinate right before the appointment.

During the scan

Your pet lies on their side or back while a small area of fur on the belly is clipped so the probe can make good contact with the skin. A warm gel is applied, and the scan itself is painless. Many pets settle well. If your pet is anxious or can't stay still, your vet will talk to you about options to keep them comfortable.

After the scan

Your vet reviews what they saw and explains it in plain language. Depending on the findings, the next steps might be treatment, a sample for testing, a follow-up scan or simply reassurance that everything looks normal. The clipped fur grows back.

Pet Ultrasound in Langley BC: Frequently Asked Questions

Is a pet ultrasound safe?

Yes. Ultrasound uses sound waves, not radiation and is considered safe for dogs, cats and pregnant pets.

Does my pet need to be sedated for an ultrasound?

Many pets do well awake because the scan is painless. Anxious or wriggly pets may need extra help to stay calm, and your vet will tell you what is best for yours.

How long does a pet ultrasound take?

The time varies with the area being scanned and your pet's temperament. Your clinic can give you a realistic estimate when you book.

How much does a pet ultrasound cost in Langley?

The cost depends on what is being scanned and whether any extras, such as a sample, are needed. Contact the clinic for an estimate for your pet.

When can a cat pregnancy ultrasound be done?

Ultrasound can usually confirm pregnancy a few weeks after mating. Ask your vet for the right timing for your cat.

Can I find out the cause of my pet's vomiting with an ultrasound?

Often, yes, or at least narrow it down. It can reveal foreign objects, inflamed intestines, organ problems or masses that don't show up on routine tests.

Do I need a referral for veterinary imaging in Langley?

Not always. If your pet is already a patient, start by booking an exam so your vet can recommend the right test.
