Services / Chronic pain

Chronic pain

Pain from arthritis or other lasting conditions can change your pet's daily life. We look for the cause and adjust care as their needs change.

What we do

We assess how your pet moves and how comfortable they are. Blood tests and X-rays can help us find the source of pain.

What's included

  • Checks for arthritis and joint pain.
  • Regular reviews of pain and treatment.
  • Medicines and other therapies for pain and swelling.
  • Advice on daily habits and support for moving around.
  • Reviews of your pet's comfort and quality of life.

We follow up to see how treatment is working and make changes when needed.

Questions

Slowing down, experiencing stiffness after resting, or hesitating on stairs are often signs of chronic pain rather than just normal aging. A full veterinary assessment can help determine your dog's comfort levels and guide appropriate management strategies.

Cats naturally hide pain very well. Instead of vocalizing, chronic pain in felines often manifests as subtle behavioural changes, such as hiding, a decreased willingness to jump onto furniture, hesitating before leaps, or changes in grooming habits.

We recommend discussing appropriate exercise durations, low-impact walking surfaces, weight management plans, and therapeutic options, such as tailored joint supplements or prescribed medications, to support their mobility and comfort.

Follow-up visits are typically scheduled more frequently at the beginning of a plan, such as every few weeks, to monitor your pet's response to treatment. Once comfort levels are stably managed, the intervals between evaluations are usually extended to every few months.

Yes, modifying your home environment can significantly support your dog or cat's quality of life. Providing supportive orthopaedic bedding, placing non-slip rugs on slick flooring, using ramps instead of steps, and helping them maintain a healthy body weight can help minimize daily joint strain.

We take pain management seriously and utilize multi-modal protocols. Your pet receives pain relief medications before the procedure begins, continuous general anesthesia during surgery, and tailored post-operative medications to help support a comfortable recovery at home.
